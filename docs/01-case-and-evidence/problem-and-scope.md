# Problem and scope

## Decision question

Is the available onboarding information clear, consistent, and testable enough
to build from?

## Problem

Teams often discover vague language, missing conditions, duplicated behavior,
and conflicting rules only after design or development begins. The rework is
expensive because the original statement, the detected problem, and the final
decision are rarely kept in one traceable chain.

This project demonstrates a controlled review method on a fictional pack. It
does not claim that a language model can approve requirements or replace the
people responsible for meaning and scope.

## Included

- Load five versioned synthetic source documents.
- Extract 50 stable requirement-like items.
- Identify candidate quality issues.
- require an exact source span for every finding.
- Propose clearer wording and clarification questions.
- Pause before any revision becomes accepted.
- Export a reviewable issue register and approved revision set.

## Excluded

- Legal or regulatory interpretation.
- Automatic modification of source documents.
- External system writes or live enterprise connectors.
- Private, personal, or real customer data.
- Production authentication, multi-tenancy, or deployment claims.
- A general-purpose document chatbot.

## Primary audiences

- Product and transformation leaders evaluating decision quality.
- Engineering leaders evaluating controls and reproducibility.
- Practitioners evaluating the review method and evidence chain.
- Recruiters evaluating the ability to connect analysis and AI engineering.
